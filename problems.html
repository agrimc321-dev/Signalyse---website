§ PROBLEMS WE SOLVE

The questions your data can answer.

Every small hospitality business is sitting on data it doesn’t have time to read. Below are six of the questions we hear most often — and the kinds of patterns we find when we look.

Six questions we answer

01

“Does rain actually hurt my sales — or does it just change them?”

Most owners assume rain hurts trade, full stop. The data often tells a more useful story. When the rain comes, footfall typically drops — but the customers who do come tend to stay longer and order more. The right response usually isn’t a discount; it’s to slow the service down, lean into the dwell, and adjust staffing for fewer-but-longer covers. We look for the temperature and rainfall thresholds where your specific business genuinely flips.

THE KIND OF FINDING WE LOOK FOR — your weather inflection points, by daypart

02

“Are my promotions actually working — or would those customers have come anyway?”

Happy hour pulls a crowd into the bar. The harder question is whether those customers would have come at 6pm instead of 5pm, with the same total spend, regardless of the promotion. A counterfactual analysis — comparing promo days to matched non-promo days — separates real lift from demand-shifting. The honest answer surprises most owners. Sometimes the promotion works. Sometimes it just rearranges the day.

THE KIND OF FINDING WE LOOK FOR — headline lift vs. net lift on every promotion you run

03

“Which products are quietly carrying the menu — and which are wasting space?”

Best-sellers by revenue and best-sellers by profit are rarely the same list. Plot volume against margin and the menu sorts itself into four quadrants: stars to protect, dogs to cut, puzzles to reprice, workhorses to leave alone. The dogs are usually the surprise — products owners assume are pulling their weight because they sell, when in fact the margin is so thin they’re losing money on every plate.

THE KIND OF FINDING WE LOOK FOR — your menu, sorted by profit not popularity

04

“When am I overstaffed — and when am I leaving money on the floor?”

Revenue-per-staff-hour, plotted across a typical week, almost always shows two windows where the rota is wrong. One where there are too many hands. One where there aren’t enough. Fixing each is worth real money, and the fix is rarely “add a shift” or “cut a shift” — it’s usually a 30-minute shift in when the existing rota starts.

THE KIND OF FINDING WE LOOK FOR — your overstaffed and understaffed hours, by weekday

05

“Does payday actually move my numbers?”

For a lot of small hospitality, week four of the month quietly underperforms — and the owners feel it without naming it. The fix isn’t a blanket promotion; it’s a targeted offer that lands on the right weekday of the right week. The trick is identifying which weekday matters for your specific customer base, because it’s not the same for a city-centre pub and a residential café.

THE KIND OF FINDING WE LOOK FOR — your monthly cycle, mapped to the UK payday calendar

06

“What does next Saturday actually look like?”

The forecast layer combines your historical pattern with the Met Office’s seven-day weather, local fixtures, school holidays, and term dates. The output is a single forward-looking number — expected covers, sales, or footfall — accurate enough to actually plan against. The technique is the same one used to forecast UK electricity demand. The variables are different. The maths is identical.

THE KIND OF FINDING WE LOOK FOR — your week ahead, in a single PDF every Sunday
